Why Do Seismologists Need Green's Functions & Moment Tensors?

A seismogram uᵢ(x, t) couples two distinct physics: (1) how the fault ruptures (source Mⱼₖ), and (2) how seismic waves propagate, refract, and reflect through Earth's structure (path impulse response Gᵢⱼ). Green's functions decouple path effects from source physics!

Why Does a Force Couple Produce a Spatial Derivative of Gᵢⱼ?

Fault slip is driven by internal opposing forces (+fⱼ and -fⱼ) separated by an infinitesimal lever arm δξₖ along direction k.

Key Intuition: Taking the point-source limit δξₖ → 0 while keeping Mⱼₖ = fⱼ δξₖ finite turns the opposing force pair into the spatial partial derivative ∂/∂ξₖ!
Diagram 3 · Conservation of Angular Momentum

Why Must Earthquakes Be Double Couples Instead of Single Couples?

Internal faulting cannot exert a net external torque on the Earth; otherwise angular momentum conservation would be violated.

Consequently, symmetry (Mⱼₖ = Mₖⱼ) reduces the 9 components of the 3×3 moment tensor to only 6 independent components!

1. Point-Source Approximation Validity

Real earthquakes rupture across finite fault areas. A finite fault can be approximated as a point-source moment tensor Mⱼₖ only when the observed seismic wavelength λ ≫ fault dimension L and epicentral distance r ≫ L.

2. Nodal Plane Ambiguity

A pure strike-slip double couple satisfies M₁₂ = M₂₁, meaning left-lateral slip on an E-W fault and right-lateral slip on a N-S fault share the exact same moment tensor! Far-field point-source data alone cannot distinguish the fault plane from the auxiliary plane.
